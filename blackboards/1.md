## Analytical Insight: Uniform Perturbation Formula

**Proposition.** Under uniform shift s_{ij} → s_{ij} + ε for all i ≠ j (diagonal unchanged):

\Delta_\text{new} = \Delta_\text{old} - \varepsilon \, (1 - H)

where H = \sum_i p_i^2 is the Herfindahl concentration index.

**Proof.**
\Delta = 1 - \sum_{i,j} s_{ij} p_i p_j

Under perturbation:
\Delta_\text{new} = 1 - \sum_{i,j} (s_{ij} + \varepsilon \mathbf{1}_{i \neq j}) p_i p_j
= \Delta_\text{old} - \varepsilon \sum_{i \neq j} p_i p_j
= \Delta_\text{old} - \varepsilon (1 - H)  \qquad \square

**Corollary.** The difference between any two researchers under uniform perturbation:

\Delta_{A,\text{new}} - \Delta_{B,\text{new}} = (\Delta_A - \Delta_B) + \varepsilon (H_A - H_B)

Hence:
|\Delta_A - \Delta_B|_\text{new} = |(\Delta_A - \Delta_B) + \varepsilon (H_A - H_B)|

The gap vanishes at \varepsilon^* = -(\Delta_A - \Delta_B)/(H_A - H_B).

## Structural Robustness

**Proposition.** \Delta_A ≈ \Delta_B is structurally robust under uniform perturbation.

With H_A = 0.2588, H_B = 0.2000, the signed gap evolves as:
\Delta_A - \Delta_B + \varepsilon(H_A - H_B) = -0.021 + 0.059\varepsilon

This vanishes at \varepsilon^* = 0.021/0.059 = 0.351 — requiring 35% perturbation of all similarities.

For any ε:
- ε < 0.351: \Delta_A < \Delta_B, gap < 0.021
- ε = 0.351: gap = 0 (exact inversion)
- ε > 0.351: \Delta_A > \Delta_B, gap grows linearly

In all cases, the gap remains small relative to the A/B-vs-C separation (~0.33).

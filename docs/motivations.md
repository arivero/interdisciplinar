# Research Motivations and Open Questions

This file lists what the project is trying to achieve. Read it at the start of
every session to orient work toward genuine research goals, not just mechanical
expansion.

## Overarching Thesis
Produce an accurate, critical review of research on interdisciplinarity in
university research, with special focus on the indicators used to measure it,
and report the current state of knowledge with explicit source recency.

## Foundational Goals
1. Build a clear map of existing interdisciplinarity indicators and what each one
   actually measures.
2. Separate conceptual claims from empirically validated claims in the literature.
3. Define a practical measurement framework usable by universities with rich
   internal data access.
4. Maintain an up-to-date bibliography so conclusions track the current research frontier.

## Open Questions (prioritized)
### 1. Can we reliably measure the impact and quality of interdisciplinary researchers?
What definitions of "impact" and "quality" remain valid across disciplinary
boundaries and over realistic time horizons?

### 2. Which concrete interdisciplinarity measurements can be done internally?
Assume full institutional access to projects, publications, staff composition,
and researcher/group budgets. Which indicators are computable, auditable, and
decision-relevant?

### 3. How should we distinguish cross-disciplinarity from polymathy?
Working hypothesis: cross-disciplinarity is not merely being conversant in many
topics; it is producing work that substantively affects multiple research areas.

## Satellite Paper Status and Aims
- Planned short letter (max 6 compiled pages): methods and concrete measurements
  for institutional internal use, leveraging complete project/article/staff/budget data.
- Purpose: provide an operational, reproducible measurement recipe rather than a
  broad conceptual survey.
- Status: not started.

## Lifecycle Rule
Every satellite paper must end in one of two states:
1. **Publication track** (P cycle) — compiled, submitted, done.
2. **Discarded** (`git rm -r`) — content salvaged to notebooks if valuable, then deleted.

No paper should linger indefinitely in draft.

## Session Priority Rule
Open questions come first. If a session can make progress on an open question,
do that. If not, targeted expansion of below-limit satellites. Never expand
a paper that's already at the page limit.

## Bibliography Cadence Rule
Because this project aims to report the state of knowledge, bibliography cycles
must run more frequently than usual. Prefer at least one `B` cycle in each
active working block (or whenever new claims are promoted into manuscripts).

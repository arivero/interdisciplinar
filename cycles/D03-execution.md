# D03 Execution: Quality Dimension Framing

Date: 2026-02-15

## Key findings from source review

### 1. Quality is a contested construct
Rafols (2019) argues that "quality does not actually exist" as an objective
attribute — it is a hybrid product of evaluation exercises. The "apparent
consensus on 'excellence' or 'impact' is based in ambiguity." This means
any attempt to measure "quality" of IDR must be explicit about what
operationalization is used.

### 2. Citation penalty is temporal, not permanent
Recent meta-evidence shows IDR receives fewer citations over 3-year windows
but achieves greater and more lasting impact over 13+ years. The "penalty"
is a diffusion lag, not a quality deficit.

### 3. Balance–citation tension
Cantone (2024) reports (citing Larivière & Gingras 2010; Wang et al. 2015)
that papers with more balanced references across disciplines get cited LESS
than papers with equal diversity but less balance. This is a direct empirical
"citation penalty for integration" — precisely the researchers our panel
identifies as integrators (high Δ, high S).

### 4. Diffusion conflation problem
Cantone (2024) notes that measures like betweenness centrality and diffusion
conflate citation count with interdisciplinarity: highly-cited work appears
more interdisciplinary simply because it has more links. Our E is a
*fraction* (not absolute count), which avoids this conflation.

### 5. Type of IDR matters for evaluation outcomes
Xiang, Romero, and Teplitskiy (2025, PNAS): knowledge-base IDR (diverse
references) correlates with HIGHER acceptance rates; topic IDR (crossing in
text/subject) correlates with LOWER acceptance rates. The panel's ability to
characterize TYPE of IDR is therefore relevant to understanding quality
assessments.

## Design decision

**Quality is NOT a fifth dimension for the taxonomy or panel.** Quality
measurement is orthogonal to interdisciplinarity measurement but subject to
specific biases when applied to IDR. The correct treatment is:

1. Add a subsection to §6 (Open Problems) discussing the quality–IDR
   tension: citation temporal bias, balance penalty, and the need for
   time-horizon awareness in evaluation.
2. Note that E avoids the diffusion-conflation problem by design (fraction,
   not count).
3. Reference the Xiang et al. finding as motivation for type-discriminating
   panels like ours.

This is a **minor content addition** (C03), not a structural change.

## Mapping to OQ1
OQ1 asks: "Can we reliably measure the impact and quality of interdisciplinary
researchers?"

Answer framing: Impact yes (via E), with the caveat that citation-based
quality measures are temporally biased against IDR. Quality itself is not
reliably measured by any single indicator, interdisciplinary or otherwise
(Rafols 2019). What the panel provides is the structural characterization
needed to *interpret* quality indicators correctly — e.g., knowing that a
researcher is an integrator (not a polymath) explains why their citations
may be delayed.
